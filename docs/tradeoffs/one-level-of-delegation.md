# One level of delegation in a chain

**Gain:** a cell delegates without blocking, and `rika.agents.inspectAll` waits up to a `waitMillis` the host clamps, so a parent can outlive the child it delegated to and use the answer. The wait ends as soon as every named child is terminal, and one that elapses reports the children as they are rather than failing.

**Cost:** delegation is one level deep. Baton's local scheduler runs at most `concurrency` Runs, four by default, and every Run in a chain holds one of those slots at the same time: a spawned child is moved to `running` the instant it is admitted rather than queued for later, and a parent holds its slot for its whole turn because nothing returns it to `queued` while a child is unsettled. A Thread's first turn also admits a Title agent. So a root, its Title, a child, and that child's own child are already four of four, and the middle agent's next turn — the one that reports what its child produced — waits for a slot that its own parent is holding until that very turn arrives. The chain cannot finish. Nothing reports it: the inner agent simply stops, its cell stays `running`, and any outer wait spends its whole budget. Waiting does not cause this and removing the wait does not avoid it; a cell that needs work from two levels down must let the outer levels return and read results from the durable transcript instead.

**Rejected:** raising the scheduler's concurrency moves the depth at which the chain stalls rather than removing the limit, and the scheduler admits oldest-first, so added capacity reaches the shallowest Runs before the deepest one that is actually blocked. Releasing a slot for the duration of a wait is the real fix and belongs with Baton's execution host rather than a binding, because a Run that yields its slot must reclaim it without losing its cell's live namespace. Blocking admission instead of polling would put a child's latency inside the parent's cell deadline, which is the failure the bounded wait exists to avoid.
